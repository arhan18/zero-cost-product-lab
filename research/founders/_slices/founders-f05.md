### F05_1. David Heinemeier Hansson (DHH)

- **name**: David Heinemeier Hansson (DHH)
- **majorProducts**: Basecamp (project management SaaS); HEY (email); Ruby on Rails (web framework); ONCE (self-hosted software incl. Campfire); Fizzy (issue tracker); books Getting Real / Rework / Remote / It Doesn't Have To Be Crazy At Work
- **productCategory**: SaaS project management, email, developer tools / web framework
- **teamSize**: Basecamp built by ~3 programmers + 3 designers; company ~62 people (2026). v1 built solo by DHH (sole developer) as a $15/hr Copenhagen contractor.
- **background**: Danish (b. Copenhagen 1979). Video-game journalist as a teen; Danish dot-com startups; Copenhagen Business School (CS + business administration). Joined 37signals as remote contractor after answering Jason Fried's PHP question on Signal v. Noise; later co-owner/CTO. Le Mans class-winning racer; Shopify board member since 2024.
- **originWhy**: 37signals was a web design consultancy failing to manage client projects with email/chat/spreadsheets. Basecamp was built summer 2003 out of 'desperate necessity' to stop embarrassing themselves; clients then wanted the tool themselves.
- **problemDiscovery**: Direct operational pain (scattered info, embarrassing client work); commercial tools (Microsoft Project, etc.) too big/enterprise. Launched 2004 as one of the first SaaS apps and defined the category.
- **validation**: Within ~1 year Basecamp out-earned the web design business; tens of thousands of companies had the same problem; 2004-2007 launched one product/year (Basecamp, Backpack, Campfire, Highrise). 2005: ~40 VCs came calling; only Jeff Bezos's self-removing-high termsheet was accepted — all others declined.
- **mvpApproach**: Built v1 alone in ~380 hours at a hard 10-hours-per-week constraint; shipped Feb 2004. Tagline 'Less Software' — fewer features, done very well, vs Microsoft Project. Internal tool first, productized on client demand.
- **timeToFirstVersion**: ~380 hours over ~5-6 months; Basecamp v1 released February 2004.
- **stack**: Ruby on Rails (he created the framework to build Basecamp). Moved off cloud to own servers (~2023) to save millions/year. 2026 side project: Omarchy, an Arch-derived Linux distro.
- **aiUsage**: 37signals experimented with AI across Fizzy/Basecamp for ~18 months and deliberately did NOT ship ('we didn't ship'); HEY launched with no AI (2025). 2026: Basecamp made 'agent accessible'; he says he was slower than Shopify's Tobi to bet on the agent discontinuity.
- **designApproach**: 'Less software.' Copywriting is design. Constraint-driven design (too little time/money/people forces prioritization). Opinionated products; a coherent single idea per product.
- **launchStrategy**: One product/year in early years; massively public writing + open-source credibility; periodic flagship releases (Basecamp 5, 2026). Product + writing instead of ads.
- **distributionChannels**: Signal v. Noise blog, 4 NYT-bestselling books, podcast, public talks, word of mouth, inbound demand. No significant paid marketing or sales force.
- **audienceBuilding**: Decades of public writing (world.hey.com/dhh, books, podcast) built a developer/SMB audience every launch (Basecamp 5, HEY, ONCE, Fizzy) drops into.
- **xStrategy**: Very active on X (@dhh) as broadcast + product-news channel; not a documented systematic acquisition loop.
- **redditStrategy**: UNKNOWN — no documented, significant Reddit strategy.
- **phHnStrategy**: HN historically core for 37signals/Rails culture; brand-driven presence, not a structured campaign.
- **pricing**: Flat, simple pricing as strategy: Basecamp one flat price (no per-seat); HEY flat $99/yr (annual-only, no free tier) + $12/user/mo domains; ONCE = buy once, run yourself. 'We don't sell you.' Anti-'NOTASAP' (no unnecessary SaaS-ification).
- **monetization**: Direct subscriptions + one-time ONCE licenses; bootstrapped and profitable with heavy dividend distributions; no ads, no data selling.
- **revenueEvidence**: 'Hundreds of millions of dollars in revenue over 20 years' (Signals, 2025); tens of millions in annual profit (Fried, Every/2025). Original 2004 Basecamp v1 (stopped selling 2010) still earns millions in profit from legacy customers.
- **userGrowthEvidence**: 20M+ people have used Basecamp globally (Elevate, 2025); ~1M users/day (DHH, 2026); HEY 'tens of thousands of customers' (dhh.dk); Rails powers GitHub, Shopify, Airbnb.
- **successFactor**: Bootstrapped profitability as a moat; small teams; constraint-led scope; radical opinionation; independence ('no one gets to tell him what to do').
- **biggestMistake**: (1) 2014 'Becoming Basecamp' consolidation cutting new dev on Highrise/Campfire/Backpack (later reversed for HEY in 2020). (2) Being slower than Shopify's CEO to bet on the AI-agent discontinuity. Reflexive: 'I don't trust myself with unlimited resources.'
- **stoppedDoing**: Client web-design work (2004-05); VC fundraising path; cloud hosting (~2023); actively selling legacy v1/highrise (frozen, kept running); new development on the four-product portfolio (2014) to focus Basecamp.
- **keptSimple**: Programmer+designer pairs; six-week cycles; no middle management; freezing legacy products rather than bloating them; flat pricing; one coherent idea per product.
- **productPhilosophy**: 'Less Software.' A product is a complete, coherent idea executed well, not an ever-expanding pile. Software should age gracefully; old versions are a service. Constraints choose what matters; independence is the prerequisite for lasting product judgment.
- **engineeringPhilosophy**: Opinionated frameworks (convention over configuration); start from real problems with beginner's mind ('Rails is more or less my first project in Ruby'); 4-hour uninterrupted blocks; a small codebase/small team beats big ones; post-cloud, own your infrastructure; agents/coding assistants are the next discontinuity.
- **lesson**: Constraints are a design engine, not a handicap — too little time/money/people forces you to choose what truly matters, and profitability (not investment) is what buys the freedom to keep choosing well over decades. (Transferable principle, not a copy directive.)

### F05_2. Jason Fried

- **name**: Jason Fried
- **majorProducts**: Basecamp; HEY; ONCE (one-time-purchase software incl. Campfire); Fizzy (issue tracker); Signal v. Noise (blog); books Getting Real / Rework / Remote / It Doesn't Have To Be Crazy At Work
- **productCategory**: SaaS project management, email, self-hosted software
- **teamSize**: 37signals ~62 people (2026), 'as high as' ~90; every product a small team, often single programmer-designer pairs. Basecamp product team ~3 programmers + 3 designers.
- **background**: Founded 37signals 1999 (Chicago) as a web design consultancy; self-described copywriter/designer, not a coder. Co-founder & CEO for 27 years. Writes a personal letter with his email to every new Basecamp customer.
- **originWhy**: Built Basecamp out of 'desperate necessity' — managing client work via email/chat/spreadsheets started embarrassing them; existing tools failed; they built an internal system that clients immediately asked to buy.
- **problemDiscovery**: Direct client-service pain as the design firm grew; tried popular tools first, none worked; client pull ('We could totally use this for our own projects!') confirmed a generalizable need across tens of thousands of companies. Basecamp defined a category.
- **validation**: Basecamp out-earned consulting within ~1 year; clients repeatedly asking for the internal tool was the initial signal; thousands of improvements since from feedback + self-use.
- **mvpApproach**: Simple, straightforward toolchain built for themselves first, then polished and shipped. 'Getting Real' method: build real products, cut scope, no fake data. Personal sign-up letter (real person, real email).
- **timeToFirstVersion**: Basecamp v1 released February 2004 (~6 months after summer-2003 start; ~380 hours coding by DHH).
- **stack**: Ruby on Rails (Basecamp was the original Rails app). After 25 years, moved infra off cloud to own premises (~2022-23), saving 'millions and millions' per year — cloud is a convenience premium a stable, expert company no longer needs. HEY and Fizzy also on Rails.
- **aiUsage**: Deliberately cautious: HEY shipped with NO AI (2025) — summarization belongs at the OS level; AI triage breaks trust; sees real value only in contextual AI (e.g., noticing a conversation went cold). Fizzy experiments with a natural-language 'do bar'. 2026: Basecamp 'agent accessible.' His line: AI makes building fast trivial — the hard part is maintenance and trust.
- **designApproach**: Galapagos principle — evolve products in isolation: never study competitors, no focus groups, no systematic A/B testing, no spreadsheets ('I've never looked at a spreadsheet and then done something because of what it told me'). Inspiration from physical products (Concept2 rower, 1963 Rolex Daytona) and architecture; software 'slides downhill' toward bloat without physical constraints; every version must get simpler at its core. Purity — first execution of an idea is closest to pure. Demos use real data, never fake.
- **launchStrategy**: Launch real product, market with writing and craft; flagship version releases (Basecamp 5) batch features for attention. 'I want our products to explain themselves. I want our success to explain ourselves.' Targets small businesses ('The Fortune 5,000,000'), not enterprise.
- **distributionChannels**: Content (blog, books, talks, podcast), word of mouth, customer success ('product explains itself'), direct letter/email access at signup. Acknowledges distribution is the hardest part for small teams without budgets or audiences.
- **audienceBuilding**: 27 years of writing + 4 bestselling books + talks; products distribute to customers with personal access to the CEO.
- **xStrategy**: Active professional presence on X and media; used for opinion and product narrative; not a documented systematic acquisition loop.
- **redditStrategy**: UNKNOWN — no documented, significant Reddit strategy.
- **phHnStrategy**: HN historically strong for 37signals launches and Rails culture; engagement via posts and podcasts rather than structured campaigns.
- **pricing**: Pricing as philosophy: Basecamp one flat monthly price, no per-seat; HEY flat $99/yr + $12/user/mo domains; ONCE = own it forever (one-time, self-hosted). 'We don't sell you.' Avoids enterprise; 'I don't care about enterprise customers. I like small, medium-sized businesses.'
- **monetization**: Subscriptions + one-time ONCE licenses; no ads/data sale; bootstrapped, consistently profitable, distributes profits. On-prem move cut costs and raised margin — 'competing against costs, not competitors.'
- **revenueEvidence**: Tens of millions in profit every year (Every, 2025); moving on-prem saves 'millions and millions' annually (TBPN Digest, 2025); Basecamp still 'growing and very profitable after 20 years' (Practical Founders, 2025).
- **userGrowthEvidence**: ~1M users/day (2026); used by millions over 23 years; 20M+ people globally (Elevate); one of the first web apps built on Rails.
- **successFactor**: Refusing VC and the unicorn path; profitability as freedom; small tight teams; craft and coherent product ideas; longevity ('27 years is more interesting than 15'); deciding on intuition because no investor needs a spreadsheet justification.
- **biggestMistake**: Largest documented organizational mistake: a 2021 leadership/policy decision caused ~30% of the company to quit overnight; he says staying principled eventually made 37signals stronger but acknowledges real damage (Leap Academy). Product-level: keeping 4 products in flight was too much — in 2014 he 'had to say enough is enough' (Basecamp Inc. rebrand). Openly warns software 'slides downhill' and admits fighting it is the hardest ongoing battle.
- **stoppedDoing**: Consulting/web design (out-earned by Basecamp in year 1); investing in four parallel products (2014); cloud hosting (~2022-23); growth targets/unicorn exits; looking at competitors entirely; shipping AI features that don't earn trust.
- **keptSimple**: Small teams, flat org (no middle management); six-week cycles; 'the thing that matters should be big, and the rest of it as thin as possible'; every version simpler at the core than the last; flat pricing; no focus groups or A/B testing.
- **productPhilosophy**: Products are complete, coherent ideas — 'a single piece, a single idea, a single concept executed beautifully.' Never compete on a competitor's feature list; evolve on your own island. Build for small businesses like yourself and stay close to them. Enough is enough: maintain and improve rather than expand and bloat.
- **engineeringPhilosophy**: Small teams build the best software; keep the decision surface thin; day-by-day course correction over multi-year planning; own your hardware once stable; engineering for maintenance because 'the hardest thing is not making something — the hardest thing is maintaining something.'
- **lesson**: Your real competition is your cost base and your own discipline to keep products simple — not other companies; long-term profitability plus independence lets you make product calls on intuition instead of on what investors would accept. (Transferable principle.)
- **evidenceGrade**: FACT

### F05_4. Rob Walling

- **name**: Rob Walling
- **majorProducts**: Drip (ecommerce email automation; sold to Leadpages 2016); HitTail (SEO app; acquired); TinySeed (bootstrapper accelerator + fund ~$59-60M, 210+ companies); MicroConf (conference/community); Startups for the Rest of Us (podcast, 800+ episodes / 15M+ downloads); books Start Small Stay Small, The SaaS Playbook, Exit Strategy

As operator/investor (2025-26): AI compresses build time (years to months) but the hard parts remain; every TinySeed founder who gets to the millions is burning out — that's the killer, not build speed.
