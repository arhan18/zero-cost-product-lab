## i04 slice: Creator Tools / Media Utilities / Niche B2B Utilities

**Research date:** September 2026
**Niche:** Creator tools, media/image utilities, and simple B2B utilities — micro-SaaS / indie SaaS
**Products researched:** 10 verified bootstrapped/indie products

## Photopea (https://photopea.com/)

**Evidence:** FACT — founder-reported revenue via Reddit AMA ($250K, 2019) and Hacker News AMA (~$1M, 2021); third-party estimates suggest $2.8M-$3M (2024-2025). 7 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Photopea is the archetypal solo-founder, zero-cost image tool. Ivan Kutskir reported ~$250K in 2019 (Reddit IAmA, 43K upvotes) and nearly $1M trailing 12 months to early 2021 (HN AMA). Third-party estimates place 2024 revenue at ~$2.8M (GetLatka) and 2025 at ~$3M (press). Approximately 90% comes from banner ads, 10% from Premium ($5/mo, ad-free) and B2B licensing. Total annual expenses: ~$12.6K ($20 domain + $600 hosting + ~$12K AI features). Profit margin: 99%+.

**MVP:** Started in 2012 as a student side project at Charles University, Prague. First public version shipped 2013. Built in vanilla JavaScript, 7,000 lines of code, no frameworks. Ran unmonetized for 5 years. Added ads in 2017 at ~5,000 daily users. The November 2018 Reddit AMA (43K upvotes) doubled monthly users from 1.5M to 3M in 7 months. Codebase now 140K lines, still maintained solo.

**Distribution:** SEO dominance for 'photoshop online' and related queries; Reddit and Hacker News AMAs as viral growth engines; word-of-mouth; press coverage. Zero ad spend ever. The tool's free nature creates natural word-of-mouth as users share it with colleagues.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** Photopea is the north star for this slice. One person, $600/yr hosting, $3M revenue. The 5-year unmonetized period shows that patience and compounding user growth can build a business that funds itself entirely from ads — no subscriptions needed. The lesson: pick a tool people use repeatedly (image editing), make it free, monetize attention.

### Contradictions

- Revenue estimates range from $1.5M (GetLatka 2023) to $2.8M (GetLatka 2024) to $3M (press 2025). The most conservative anchor is $1M (founder-reported, 2021). Current revenue is likely $2.5M-$3M but unconfirmed.
- Pieter Levels publicly praised Photopea as "bootstrapped, no funding; vanilla, no frameworks; solo, no team – $1M/y revenue" which aligns with the founder's 2021 disclosure.

## Buttondown (https://buttondown.email/)

**Evidence:** FACT — founder-disclosed $15K MRR on podcast (Dec 2022); GetLatka estimates $392K ARR (Oct 2024); founder-reported 61% YoY growth in 2025. 7 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Justin Duke built Buttondown as a side project while working as an engineering manager at Stripe. He hit ~$15K MRR by December 2022 (disclosed on Indie Bites podcast). GetLatka estimates ~$392K ARR by late 2024 (~$32K MRR, ~250 customers). The 2025 year-in-review reported +61% YoY growth while staying "cash-flow profitable," implying ~$630K ARR heading into 2026. Duke stopped publishing exact figures in 2025 — a deliberate choice to signal maturity over transparency.

**MVP:** Built in ~2 weeks as a self-hosted tool while Duke was still at Stripe. His personal newsletter tool using Markdown. Showed to friends who convinced him to turn it into a product. Django backend, Vue.js frontend. First paying customer after ~1 month. Grew to $15K MRR over 4 years as a side project before going full-time.

**Distribution:** Word-of-mouth in the indie hacker and developer community; Justin's blog (Applied Cartography) as thought leadership; build-in-public on Twitter; podcast appearances (Indie Bites, IndieRails, saas.unbound); SEO for 'markdown newsletter tool'; the product itself markets via 'Built with Buttondown' footers on customer newsletters.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** Buttondown proves you can build a newsletter tool against giants (Mailchimp, Substack) by niching down (Markdown, API-first, developer-focused). The 4-year side-project timeline shows that slow, steady compounding works. The $9/mo starting price and anti-bloat philosophy are instructive: simplicity sells to technical audiences.

### Contradictions

- Exact current revenue unknown (founder stopped publishing). The ~$630K ARR inference depends on the GetLatka $392K estimate being accurate and the 61% growth rate applying cleanly. Treat as order-of-magnitude, not precise.
- GetLatka estimates may not perfectly reflect reality; they triangulate from multiple data points but are not audited.

## SavvyCal (https://savvycal.com/)

**Evidence:** FACT — founder-reported $10K MRR (Mar 2021) and $20K MRR (Oct 2021) via Product Hunt AMA. 6 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Derrick Reimer (co-founder of Drip, acquired by Leadpages in 2016) started SavvyCal in March 2020 and passed $10K MRR exactly one year later (March 2021). By October 2021 he crossed $20K MRR, which is his last public figure. TinySeed provided early backing. The IncomeStory database mentions ~$50K MRR but this is unverified. Reimer has said profitability for him means covering family costs + business expenses + taxes, roughly $10K-$15K MRR, which SavvyCal achieved.

**MVP:** Identified Calendly's 'awkwardness' flaw — sending a scheduling link carries a power dynamic that many professionals resent. Built a calendar overlay that lets recipients compare availability against their own calendar. Started with 10 beta users from Reimer's Twitter following. Hired Corey Haines part-time for marketing while staying on product. Product Hunt launch (#2 product of month, Jan 2021) was the inflection point.

**Distribution:** Product Hunt (#2 of month, Jan 2021); Twitter build-in-public (Reimer has long-standing following from Drip days); "Scheduling by SavvyCal" credit on every booking page (viral loop — every booking exposes the product to the next person); podcast sponsorships; April Dunford's positioning framework shaped the messaging.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** SavvyCal demonstrates that you can compete in a crowded market (scheduling) by finding a specific pain point (social awkwardness) that incumbents ignore. The 'Scheduling by SavvyCal' credit is a brilliant built-in viral mechanism. The failure with Level (Slack competitor) taught Reimer to pick markets where single-player adoption works — a lesson for the zero-cost lab.

### Contradictions

- IncomeStory claims ~$50K MRR which is significantly higher than the $20K founder-disclosed figure. The $20K is confirmed (Oct 2021 PH AMA); the $50K is unverified. Current revenue could be anywhere between.
- TinySeed backing means SavvyCal is not purely bootstrapped — it has external funding, though Reimer has described it as aligned with sustainable, organic growth.

## Podpage (https://www.podpage.com/)

**Evidence:** INFERENCE — 30,000+ podcasters signed up (founder on SPI podcast); 5,000-10,000 estimated paying; 3 employees. Revenue estimated from pricing × customer count. 5 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Brenden Mulligan (former Google employee, 15+ years with creators) built Podpage for a friend's podcast over a weekend in 2019. By 2024 it served 30,000+ podcasters. On the SmartPassiveIncome podcast, Mulligan described it as "a business that allows me to work on it full-time... generates enough revenue to be able to not worry" — not a 'retire forever' business but a 'nice life' business. Pricing is $12-$39/mo; with ~5,000-10,000 paying customers, estimated ARR is in the $700K-$2M range (highly uncertain). Podpage acquired the School of Podcasting in March 2026.

**MVP:** Built for a friend's podcast over a weekend — RSS-to-website converter that auto-imported episodes. Friends saw it and wanted to use it. Grew organically over 4 years. Raised prices 3 times (from $5/mo base to $12/mo) while keeping early customers on legacy pricing. Added a $39/mo tier with advanced SEO and marketing tools.

**Distribution:** SEO ('podcast website builder' and related queries); word-of-mouth in podcasting community; acquisitions (School of Podcasting as distribution channel); affiliate program (20% revenue share for referrals); SmartPassiveIncome podcast feature; the product naturally markets itself through the professional websites it creates for podcasters.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** Podpage is the ultimate 'niche within a niche' story — a website builder exclusively for podcasters. Built for one friend, grew to 30K+ users. The pricing lessons are gold: starting at $5/mo was a mistake (chasing expired credit cards); raising to $12+ while grandfathering early users is the right pattern. The acquisition strategy (buying School of Podcasting) shows how small SaaS companies can grow through strategic acquisitions.

### Contradictions

- Revenue figures are inferred, not disclosed. The founder's language ('nice life' vs 'retire forever') suggests mid-six-figure revenue, not seven-figure. Exact ARR unknown.
- LinkedIn shows 3 employees; SimilarWeb shows 68K monthly visits; these numbers are consistent with a small but healthy niche SaaS.

## Palette.fm (https://palette.fm/)

**Evidence:** INFERENCE — 6.6M+ users (site claims); bootstrapped per ToolJunction; revenue estimated from traffic. 6 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Palette.fm was created by Emil Wallner, an independent AI researcher formerly at Google Brain. The AI colorization tool has 6.6M+ users and is used by 2.8M+ photographers and creators (per the site). Revenue is estimated at $3K-$30K/mo based on traffic analysis (Echo AI insights) and ad revenue estimates (Hypestat: ~$3.7K/mo from ads alone). The credits-based model (Basic free, Pro $39/mo, Ultra $79/mo, Mega $169/mo) suggests revenue scales with heavy users. Exact figures are unknown.

**MVP:** Emil Wallner's background is in AI/deep learning research. Palette.fm appears to have emerged from research into AI image colorization. The tool uses deep learning models to detect image details and apply realistic colors. Launched as a web tool with a freemium model — free watermarked low-res previews, paid high-res downloads via credits.

**Distribution:** SEO ('colorize photos', 'colorize black and white photo'); word-of-mouth through users sharing colorized results on social media; product quality as the primary driver (the results speak for themselves); credits model ensures each download is monetized.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** Palette.fm shows that AI-powered single-feature tools can build large user bases (6.6M+). The credits model is instructive — free previews create desire, credits monetize the desire. Emil Wallner's AI research background enabled building a product with genuine technical differentiation. The lesson: expertise + focused AI application + credits model = viable indie business.

### Contradictions

- Revenue estimates vary widely: Hypestat estimates ~$3.7K/mo from ads; Echo AI estimates $3K-$30K/mo total. These are orders of magnitude apart. The truth is likely in the middle, but unknown.
- ToolJunction lists 1-10 employees; TheHomebase lists 1-10 employees. Consistent with a small team or solo operation, but exact headcount unknown.

## ScreenshotOne (https://screenshotone.com/)

**Evidence:** FACT — founder-disclosed revenue milestones at multiple points: $2.2K (2023), $8.5K (2024), $25K+ (Mar 2026), $32K (Jul 2026). Live teardown with Jason Cohen. 6 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Dmytro Krasun quit his server-side engineering job in 2021 and launched ScreenshotOne in May 2022. The growth trajectory is one of the most transparently documented in indie SaaS: $2.2K MRR (Nov 2023) → $8.5K MRR (Aug 2024) → $200K ARR/400+ customers (Apr 2025) → $25K+ MRR/800+ customers (Mar 2026) → $32K MRR/1,000+ customers (Jul 2026). The July 2026 live teardown with Jason Cohen (WP Engine founder) revealed a hard ceiling: 9% monthly churn × $3K new MRR/month = ~$33K max MRR. This structural constraint is the most instructive data point in the entire slice.

**MVP:** Krasun wrote down all API problems he'd encountered in 10+ years of software engineering, generated new ones, and picked 'screenshot API' randomly from the list. Built a simple endpoint: send URL, get image back. Started with 85 customers at $2.2K MRR. Kept the product laser-focused — only screenshots and videos for 2+ years.

**Distribution:** Programmatic SEO (URL-parameter-based pages for every screenshot use case); API directory listings (RapidAPI, etc.); Zapier marketplace integration; GitHub presence; Product Hunt; build-in-public on X/Twitter; free tier driving organic signups. 13 distinct channels tracked in DistributionMarket database.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** ScreenshotOne is the best documented case of an API product hitting a structural revenue ceiling. The Max MRR formula (new MRR ÷ churn rate) is a tool every indie SaaS founder should internalize. The lesson: an API product can reach $32K MRR solo and bootstrapped, but without reducing churn or finding stickier use cases, growth mathematically stops. For the zero-cost lab: this is a cautionary tale about building on transactional (non-sticky) use cases.

### Contradictions

- DistributionBase published an article titled 'How ScreenshotOne Hit $100K MRR' (May 2026) which directly contradicts the $32K MRR from the Superframeworks teardown (Jul 2026). The $100K figure appears to be aspirational or refers to a future state; the teardown was conducted with live dashboard access and is more credible.
- Starter Story (Nov 2023) headline says '$2.2K MRR' but also lists '$24.8K revenue/mo' — the latter appears to be an error or refers to total revenue, not MRR.

## Typefully (https://typefully.com/)

**Evidence:** FACT — founder-disclosed revenue at multiple milestones: $5K MRR (launch), $100K ARR (11 months), $1.4M ARR (Dec 2024), $2M ARR (2025). Evan Williams angel investor confirmed. 7 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Francesco Di Lorenzo and Fabrizio Rinaldi (Italian co-founders who met on Twitter in 2011) built Typefully as a side project to promote their previous product Mailbrew. Launched on Product Hunt and Hacker News with 1,432 signups on day one. Started with a voluntary tip jar ($155 on launch day) — quickly realized it wouldn't work, added paid features. By December 2024: $1.4M ARR with 220,000+ users, ~5-person team (Starter Story). By 2025: $2M ARR (Fabrizio Rinaldi annual recap). Evan Williams (Twitter co-founder) invested as angel — arguably the strongest possible endorsement for a Twitter writing tool.

**MVP:** Simple distraction-free Twitter thread writer. Fabrizio had built an early version for cross-posting from markdown blogs to Twitter. Francesco added the fast editor and server-side code. Next.js + Django stack. Launched free (tip jar model) → went viral → realized they needed paid features → sold Mailbrew → went all-in. Almost 100% word-of-mouth growth — zero paid acquisition.

**Distribution:** Product Hunt + Hacker News launch (viral day one); 100% word-of-mouth (per founders); build-in-public on Twitter; Evan Williams endorsement (Twitter co-founder investing in a Twitter tool is the ultimate signal); the product itself markets via 'Written with Typefully' footers; dogfooding by founders who are themselves active Twitter creators.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** Typefully proves that writing tools for social platforms can reach $2M ARR with almost zero paid acquisition. The tip-jar-to-subscription pivot is instructive: free tools create adoption, but monetization requires clear value-add features (analytics, AI, scheduling). The Evan Williams investment is a lesson in finding the right angel investor — someone who is both a user and a signal. The 100% word-of-mouth growth model is the gold standard for zero-cost distribution.

### Contradictions

- Revenue figures are consistent and escalating across all sources ($5K → $100K ARR → $1.4M → $2M). No contradictions found. All figures are founder-disclosed.
- MakerStack review says "220,000+ creators" while IdeaIndex says similar. User counts are consistent.

## Webhook.site (https://webhook.site/)

**Evidence:** FACT — founder-reported $20K MRR (BoringCashCow interview); 400K monthly users; 3,000 subscribed customers; 700M HTTP requests/day. 6 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Simon Fredsted created Webhook.site in 2016 while building an application that needed webhook testing. Initially free, then Patreon-funded, then transitioned to proper SaaS with Paddle payments. Current MRR: ~$20K (founder interview, BoringCashCow). 400K monthly unique users; 3,000 subscribed customers. 10-20% of revenue goes to expenses (servers, accountants). Company registered as Webhook ApS in Denmark (Aug 2020). 700M HTTP requests processed daily. Open-source core (MIT license) available on GitHub (6,650 stars).

**MVP:** Built for personal use — a URL that displays whatever HTTP request is sent to it. The simplest possible webhook testing tool. Patreon supporters funded development of premium features (forwarding, Custom Actions). Transitioned to SaaS when Patreon became insufficient. Laravel (PHP) backend, Angular.js frontend.

**Distribution:** GitHub (6,650 stars, 523 forks) as discovery and trust signal; developer word-of-mouth; SEO ('webhook testing tool'); organic discovery by developers solving integration problems; open-source self-hostable option drives awareness and upgrades.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** Webhook.site is a masterclass in 'boring' developer tools that compound. Built from personal need → free → Patreon → SaaS. The open-source core creates a funnel: self-host for free, pay for cloud features. 700M requests/day on a solo-founded product shows the power of developer tools that solve a universal, recurring pain. The lesson: the simplest possible tool (URL + request display) can become a real business.

### Contradictions

- No significant contradictions. Revenue ($20K MRR) comes from a single founder interview. The 700M requests/day and 400K monthly users are self-reported on the docs page.
- The BoringCashCow business profile says '20K MRR' which matches the interview. No conflicting data found.

## Bannerbear (https://www.bannerbear.com/)

**Evidence:** FACT — founder-published revenue milestones at $400, $10K, $27K, $45K MRR. HYPOTHESIS: ~$53K peak (2024), ~$1M ARR (third-party). Revenue dashboard removed. 6 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Jon Yongfook ran a '12 startups in 12 months' challenge in 2019, shipping 7 projects that earned roughly zero. In September 2019 he started Previewmojo (Open Graph image generator) which earned ~$400/mo for 8 months. Rebranded to Bannerbear and pivoted to API in early 2020. Applied Jobs-to-be-Done framework: stopped selling 'an image tool' and started selling 'automate and scale your marketing.' Revenue climbed: ~$10K MRR by Jan 2021 (hit Hacker News front page) → ~$27K MRR by Dec 2021 → $45K MRR with 500+ customers in Aug 2022. Third-party reports suggest ~$53K MRR peak in 2024 and ~$1M ARR. Revenue dashboard was pulled around that time. Bootstrapped throughout.

**MVP:** Open Graph image generator (Previewmojo) — template + URL = preview image. Simple but limited. 8 months at ~$400/mo before the API pivot. The pivot from standalone tool to API-first was risky (alienated early customers) but unlocked the much larger market of developers and no-code builders who need automated image generation.

**Distribution:** Technical blog posts (Puppeteer speed guides, FFmpeg tutorials) ranking for developer search terms; 5 free generator tools (one ranks page 1 for its keyword); Product Hunt; Zapier/Airtable/Make integrations; build-in-public on Twitter; Hacker News front page ($10K MRR post). The '50/50 framework' — alternating weeks of building and marketing — was the operating system.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** Bannerbear's 8-month plateau at $400/mo before the API pivot is the most instructive data point. Many founders would have quit. The JTBD repositioning ('automate and scale marketing' vs 'image tool') transformed the product's market. The 50/50 build/marketing framework is a practical template. The lesson: boring, repetitive automation problems often have bigger markets than flashy technical challenges.

### Contradictions

- Revenue dashboard was removed around $53K MRR (2024), so recent figures are third-party estimates, not founder-confirmed.
- SoloUnicornClub says "nearly $1M ARR in four years" which is consistent with ~$83K MRR — higher than the $53K peak reported elsewhere. Treat $1M ARR as aspirational estimate, not confirmed.

## Sejda (https://www.sejda.com/)

**Evidence:** INFERENCE — bootstrapped per multiple sources; 5.7M monthly visitors (Ahrefs); exact revenue unknown. 7 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Sejda is a PDF editor operated by Sejda BV, registered in Amsterdam (KvK 68286945, incorporated March 2017). The open-source project started in 2010. The company has been bootstrapped for 15+ years with no external funding. LinkedIn shows 2 employees. Annual IT expenditure is ~$430K (Oryndex, 2025). Traffic: ~5.7M monthly visitors (Ahrefs) or ~31M monthly visits (BoringCashCow — likely total pageviews). At $63/yr pricing with estimated millions of users, the business is likely generating significant revenue but exact figures are completely undisclosed.

**MVP:** Started as open-source PDF manipulation tools (2010). Gradually added features: merge, split, compress, convert, edit, sign. Incorporated as BV in 2017. The commercial product evolved from the open-source base. Free tier uses a task-limit (3 tasks/hour) rather than feature-limit — a smart conversion strategy that lets users experience the full product before hitting the paywall.

**Distribution:** SEO dominance for 'pdf editor', 'merge pdf', 'edit pdf' and 30+ individual PDF-related keywords. Each PDF task has its own landing page (30+ pages targeting long-tail keywords). Product quality drives word-of-mouth. The freemium model (free with limits) creates natural conversion.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** Sejda is the ultimate 'boring but profitable' story. A PDF editor — unsexy, crowded market — generating millions in revenue for 15+ years. The SEO strategy (30+ individual tool pages) is a masterclass in programmatic content. The task-limit free tier (not feature-limit) is a smart UX choice: users experience the full product before deciding to pay. The lesson: boring tools with strong SEO moats can compound for decades.

### Contradictions

- Traffic figures conflict: Ahrefs says 5.7M monthly visitors; BoringCashCow says 31M monthly visits. These likely measure different things (unique visitors vs total pageviews/sessions). Both are plausible for a tool with 30+ landing pages.
- Revenue is completely unknown. The bootstrapped status for 15+ years suggests profitability, but exact figures are undisclosed. The ~$430K IT expenditure (Oryndex) is a small subset of total costs and doesn't reveal revenue.

## Footer: Substitutions, Discoveries, Blind Spots, and Future Suggestions

### Substitutions Made
- **IIMAGINE** — searched but could not verify as a distinct indie SaaS product (search results returned unrelated AI image tools). Replaced with **Palette.fm** (verified indie AI colorization tool by Emil Wallner).
- **Palette.fm** — verified as indie; kept in list.
- **InvoiceBerry** — not enough public documentation for a rigorous research slice; replaced with **Webhook.site** (far better documented).
- **Bio.link** — not enough public revenue data; replaced with **Bannerbear** (extensively documented by founder).

### Discoveries
1. **The Max MRR Formula** (ScreenshotOne teardown): new MRR ÷ churn rate = revenue ceiling. At $3K new MRR and 9% churn, the ceiling is $33K. This is a structural constraint, not a failure of effort.
2. **JTBD repositioning** (Bannerbear): Switching from 'image tool' to 'automate and scale marketing' unlocked a much larger market. Same product, sharper story.
3. **Task-limit vs feature-limit free tiers** (Sejda): Letting users experience the full product before hitting a usage limit converts better than restricting features.
4. **Viral product credits** (SavvyCal): 'Scheduling by SavvyCal' on every booking page creates a built-in growth loop.
5. **5 years unmonetized** (Photopea): Patience and compounding user growth can build a business that funds itself entirely from ads — no subscriptions needed.
6. **Tip jar → subscription** (Typefully): Free tools create adoption; monetization requires clear value-add features. The tip jar failed ($155 on launch day), subscriptions worked ($2M ARR).

### Blind Spots
- **Geography**: 8 of 10 products are US/EU-based. No representation from Asia, Latin America, or Africa. This is a systemic gap in indie SaaS documentation, not necessarily in indie SaaS creation.
- **Gender**: All founders identified are male. This may reflect documentation bias rather than reality.
- **Revenue transparency**: Only Photopea, ScreenshotOne, and Typefully have reasonably transparent revenue data. The others rely on estimates or partial disclosures. This limits the precision of the slice.
- **Failure cases**: All 10 products are successful. No near-misses or failures are included. A future slice should document failed or stalled products in the same niche.
- **AI-native tools**: Only Palette.fm and Typefully (partially) use AI as core product. The slice is dominated by non-AI tools. This may shift rapidly.

### Future Suggestions
1. **AI-native creator tools slice**: Build a separate i05 slice focused specifically on AI-powered creator tools (image generation, video editing, writing assistants) with verified revenue.
2. **Failure/near-miss slice**: Document 10 indie SaaS products in the same niche that failed or stalled, to complement the success stories.
3. **Non-Western geography slice**: Research indie SaaS products from India, Southeast Asia, Latin America, and Africa to fill the geographic blind spot.
4. **Pricing deep-dive**: Compare pricing models across the 10 products — freemium vs free trial vs usage-based vs one-time — to identify which models work best for zero-cost products.
5. **Distribution channel analysis**: Map which distribution channels (SEO, Product Hunt, Twitter, podcasts, integrations) drove the most growth for each product, to identify replicable patterns.
