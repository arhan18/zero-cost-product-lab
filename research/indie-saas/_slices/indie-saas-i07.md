## i07 slice: Feedback / Changelog / Forms — Collecting Input and Closing the Loop

**Research date:** September 2026
**Niche:** Forms, surveys, feedback widgets, voting boards, roadmaps, changelogs — indie SaaS
**Products researched:** 10 verified products (9 bootstrapped/indie, 1 funded-but-profitable contrast)

---

## Tally.so (https://tally.so/)

**Evidence:** FACT — founder-reported revenue at every milestone ($5K 2021 → $100K MRR 2024 → $338K MRR Oct 2025 → $4.3M ARR end-2025). 7 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Marie Martens and Filip Minev (Belgium) grew Tally from $0 to ~$4M ARR fully bootstrapped in 5 years. The trail is unusually complete: $1K MRR (Mar 2021), $10K (Feb 2022), $60K (May 2023), $100K MRR (Feb 2024), $150K (Nov 2024), $2M ARR (Feb 2025), $3M (Jun 2025), $4.3M ARR (end-2025). Team of 8 (4 full-time + 4 part-time). Roughly 2% of 800,000+ users pay €20/mo for Pro — the free tier is the acquisition channel, not a discount.

**MVP:** Notion-like form builder built summer 2020 in ~6 weeks (reusing prior-project code). Shared Sep 2020; gathered ~1,000 users via feedback-seeking outreach to no-code communities. Pro at $9/mo added Nov 2020. Product Hunt launch Mar 2021 (#4 of day) doubled users 1.5K → 3K.

**Distribution:** Product-led flywheel — every free form carries a "Made with Tally" badge. Pre-launch feedback outreach (not selling) into no-code communities. Product Hunt. Comparison SEO (Typeform/Jotform/Google Forms alternatives). Zero paid marketing, ever.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** The north star of this slice. Unlimited-free within fair use + 2% conversion + badge virality + a duo running it for 3 years = $4M ARR with 8 people. The lesson: make the free tier the marketing department.

### Contradictions

- OperatorBook (Jul 2026) reports $4.3M ARR end-2025; SaaSDecoded (Apr 2026) headlines "$5M ARR" — likely projection/rounding vs the founder figure. Prefer $4.3M.
- Team size reported as 3 (Mar 2024) vs 8 (mid-2025) — consistent with hiring after $2M ARR, not contradictory.

## Canny (https://canny.io/)

**Evidence:** FACT — founder-reported $100K+ ARR (2018), $45K MRR (2019), $1M ARR (Sep 2020, team of 7, $0 raised, inbound-only). No disclosure since 2020. 7 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Andrew Rasmussen (ex-Facebook React team engineer) and Sarah Hum (ex-Facebook designer), a couple, quit Meta, launched on Product Hunt in March 2017, hit ramen profitability (~$50K/yr) in 7 months as digital nomads, $100K+ ARR at ~80% profit by March 2018, $45K MRR by Aug 2019, and $1M ARR by September 2020 — bootstrapped, zero outbound sales, team of 7, remote across 3 continents. Current revenue UNKNOWN.

**MVP:** Simple feedback board with voting and statuses. Early pivot from consumer-facing ("give users a say in roadmaps") to B2B SaaS workflow. First customers from the PH launch (thousands of visits, 350+ trials, dozens paying).

**Distribution:** Product Hunt launch; inbound-only ever (founders state "never done outbound sales"); design quality as acquisition (customers switch from competitors for design); React Native team's usage as social proof; founder blog transparency posts.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** The reference case that feedback SaaS itself bootstraps: engineer + designer, 7 months to ramen, $1M ARR in 3.5 years. Tracked-user pricing (25 free) is the conversion mechanic worth copying — charge for reach, not seats.

### Contradictions

- Tracxn lists Canny "unfunded" yet names "Y Combinator, Samsung NEXT" as investors. Resolution: Canny completed YC Startup School (free online course, founder blog, 2017) — NOT the funded batch. Founders repeatedly state $0 raised. Samsung NEXT reference is unverified aggregator noise.

## Frill (https://frill.co/)

**Revenue:** Founded 2019 by Mike Hill (Sydney serial bootstrapper: Curator.io, Juuno) and Thomas Garrood (CTO, Laguna Beach). Team of ~6. Bundles board + roadmap + announcements in one widget from $25/mo with unlimited admins and unlimited tracked users — the anti-Canny price position. Still shipping actively (June 2026 release notes, EU hosting Oct 2025, MCP server 2026). No acquisition found — the rumor is UNCONFIRMED.

**MVP:** Lean feedback board + voting + roadmap + announcements widget; "launch lean, listen hard, compound" — the same content-first, no-investor playbook Hill used on prior SaaS.

**Distribution:** Content/SEO (alternative-vs-Canny/Beamer/HelloNext/Upvoty pages, feedback guides); founder portfolio cross-promotion; widget-embed virality; changelog-driven trust.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** Bundling three tools into one $25/mo widget with unlimited admins is a positioning masterclass for outflanking per-user incumbents. Co-founder's public "no kick-ass AI use case in 5 years" stance is a useful restraint signal.

## Featurebase (https://featurebase.app/)

**Evidence:** FACT — founder-reported $100K ARR with 163 customers (Sep 2023) and $1M ARR crossed (Jan 2026, 4x revenue, 6x profit, $0 raised, team of 6). 7 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Three self-taught Tallinn founders (Bruno Hiis, Robi Rohumaa, Markus Palm — one previously hit $20K MRR at age 14) struggled as a side project until a polished early-2022 relaunch ($1.2K MRR end-2022 → $2.5K three months later), crossed $100K ARR Sep 2023, turned down 3 acquisition offers, then 4x'd to $1M ARR in 2025 while launching 4 new product lines (support inbox, Fibi AI agent, workflows, mobile app). Powers Raycast, Lovable, n8n, beehiiv. Per-seat pricing ($29–$99) undercuts Canny's per-tracked-user model.

**MVP:** Side-project voting board; the early-2022 "sleeker, more polished version" plus higher-tier features unlocked willingness to pay — nearly 20 new customers in the final month before $2.5K MRR.

**Distribution:** Build-in-public milestone blogging ($1K → $2.5K → $100K ARR posts double as operating manual); rejected-acquisitions as PR; startup program (86% off); AI-startup word-of-mouth; dogfooding; .com acquisition.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** $0 to $1M ARR bootstrapped in 5 years with 6 people — and the pricing lesson is gold: raising Growth $35→$49 and Premium $79→$99 changed nothing in signups ("highest impact, low-effort change"). Per-seat vs per-tracked-user framing is directly reusable.

### Contradictions

- GetLatka $54.5K ARR (2024) vs founder $1M ARR (Jan 2026): sequential, not contradictory — 2025 was the 4x breakout year on new product lines. Aggregator data lags; prefer founder figures.

## Fillout (https://www.fillout.com/)

**Revenue:** Dominic Whyte (ex-Retool; founded Cheer, Sequoia-backed, acquired by Retool 2020) and Antony Toron (CTO) launched a simple form builder in 2022, reached profitability within ~2 years, then announced the seed alongside 2.0 (scheduling, payments, workflows, doc gen). 100,000+ teams, 15 people. Exact ARR UNKNOWN. Thesis: depth over simplicity — unlimited seats on every plan, meter on responses ($0 free/1,000 → $15 → $40 → $75 unlimited).

**MVP:** Simple builder with 50+ native integrations; monetized early; 2.0 expanded into a data-intake platform.

**Distribution:** Retool-ecosystem credibility; elite angel network as distribution; template + comparison SEO; integration marketplace compounding.

But the negative-burn discipline (more cash than raised) makes Fillout the closest funded analog to zero-cost ops. Unlimited-seats pricing is worth stealing.

## Sleekplan (https://sleekplan.com/)

**Evidence:** FACT on bootstrap/operation (company about page: "still bootstrapped, still independent," profitable since ~10 customers); revenue figure UNKNOWN (only a low-confidence $85K aggregator model). 7 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Marco Graf (Munich) built a feedback board as an internal tool for a prior SaaS; other teams wanted it; full focus from early 2020, public launch June 2020, AppSumo deal Sep 2021 seeded the base. Now thousands of teams (Ooma, McKinsey, Mercedes-Benz, N26, Decathlon), 1.3M+ monthly users reached, 95K+ monthly feedback signals, ~5-person remote-async team. Flat pricing: Indie $0 free forever → Starter $13 → Business $38 (most expensive self-serve) vs UserVoice's $699/mo.

**MVP:** Internal board (voting, comments, statuses) polished into an in-app chat-style widget + standalone pages + iFrames — "make feedback as easy as chat" was the founding UX bet.

**Distribution:** AppSumo launch for early traction; alternative-vs-incumbent SEO; widget virality (1.3M reached); the company's own public roadmap as a live demo; EU SaaS word-of-mouth.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** Profitable since ~10 customers on $13–$38 flat pricing is the whole-loop economics lesson: unlimited feedback/subscribers even free, meter on seats and AI credits. Internal-tool-to-product remains the cheapest possible validation.

## Nolt (https://nolt.io/)

**Evidence:** INFERENCE — no founder-disclosed revenue; founder/funding record disputed between primaries-adjacent and aggregators. 6 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** A ~2-person operation (site footer: Canada + Switzerland, ©2018–2026) selling radical simplicity: one brandable board, voting, self-updating Kanban roadmap — deliberately never becoming a platform (the changelog module famously never shipped). Per-board flat pricing ($29 Essential / $69 Pro / Enterprise custom) with unlimited users, content, and admins on every plan — and notably NO free plan (10-day trial only) as a support-burden filter. Secondary estimates put revenue in the low tens of thousands $/yr.

**MVP:** Single clean board (submit, vote, statuses) + roadmap + Slack/Discord/Jira/Trello/GitHub integrations, embeddable in under an hour.

**Distribution:** "Most recommended" startup word-of-mouth; integrations as distribution; custom importers from competitors as a switching funnel; customer stories.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** The restraint case — no free tier, per-board metering, 2 people, years of stability. Refusing free users to protect support capacity is the inverse of Tally's model and works at micro-scale. Staying small on purpose is a valid terminal state, not a failure.

## Saber Feedback (https://saberfeedback.com/)

**Evidence:** FACT — founder-documented full lifecycle: side project (2011) → full-time on savings (2015) → profitable (~1 yr) → ~$5K MRR ceiling → £100K sale (2020). 6 sources reviewed.

**Revenue:** Matt Bearman (UK freelance dev) launched visual-feedback tool BugMuncher as a side project in 2011 (renamed Saber Feedback), went full-time in 2015, hit profitability — then stagnated at an equilibrium he honestly documented: never broke ~$5K MRR. In 2020 he sold to follower Steve (Barbary Software) for ~£100K (~$134K, ~3x annual gross profit, implying ~$44K ARR) and confirmed it a year later. Still active under Barbary as a "stable, self-funded, low stress" company. Current pricing $49/$99/$249 (unlimited pageviews + feedback, metered on forms); 30-day trial, no free tier.

**MVP:** JS feedback-button snippet with screenshot capture for QA/bug reports; nights-and-weekends build; savings-funded full-time year.

**Distribution:** Transparent build-in-public blogging (the side-project-to-profitable series); indie-founder reputation; gov/university/enterprise word-of-mouth; "not a CX platform" anti-positioning; the founder-to-follower sale itself.

**Takeaway for zero-cost lab:** The only complete lifecycle in the slice — including the exit. Two lessons: (1) the equilibrium confession (growth = churn at ~$5K MRR; boredom because he wasn't his own customer — "broke the first rule... I wasn't dogfooding") is the churn-ceiling honesty every lab builder needs; (2) 3x-profit micro-exits to aligned solo buyers are a real, dignified terminal outcome.

### Contradictions

- None material. £100K figure consistent across founder posts (3x profit ≈ 3x revenue at near-zero overheads). Buyer terms (~£110K after FX) disclosed transparently.

## Footer: Substitutions, Discoveries, Blind Spots, and Future Suggestions

### Substitutions Made

- **10th slot: Saber Feedback selected over UserJot.** UserJot (solo-bootstrapped, unlimited-users-free, Tally's own Filip Minev as testimonial) was the runner-up but its founder is unnamed in verifiable sources and it has no disclosed revenue history — Saber won on primary-source depth (full lifecycle + exit price on the founder's own blog).
- No other substitutions: the 6 assigned products were all real and researchable; Fillout verified as seed-funded (not bootstrapped) and kept as the funded-but-profitable contrast with the verdict stated plainly.
- **Frill acquisition rumor: investigated, no evidence found** — product actively shipping (June 2026 notes), founders posting, enterprise expansion (EU hosting, SOC 2 track). Recorded as UNCONFIRMED, not as fact.

### Discoveries

1. **The badge flywheel (Tally):** every free form recruits its next user — free tier as marketing department, converting ~2% of 800K+ users to €20/mo.
2. **Tracked-user vs per-seat vs per-board metering:** Canny (tracked users) vs Featurebase (seats) vs Nolt (boards) vs Tally/Fillout (responses) — four different answers to "what to meter," each shaping incentives differently.
3. **The equilibrium confession (Saber, $5K MRR ceiling) + the Max MRR formula (ScreenshotOne, i04):** churn ceilings are structural, and founders who publish them honestly give the lab its most valuable data.
4. **Consultancy-funded bootstrap (FeatureOS, $2,378/yr published infra):** client cash flow as zero-cost venture capital, with radical cost transparency as marketing.
5. **No-free-tier as a filter (Nolt):** refusing free users to protect a 2-person support load — the viable inverse of product-led growth at micro-scale.
6. **Price-rise inelasticity (Featurebase $35→$49, signups unchanged):** indie tools systematically underprice; the highest-ROI change is often a pricing page edit.
7. **3x-profit micro-exits (Saber, ~£100K):** aligned solo-buyer acquisitions are a real terminal outcome for sub-$5K-MRR tools.

### Blind Spots

- **Gender:** all named founders are male except Marie Martens (Tally) and Sarah Hum (Canny) — both co-founding with partners. No solo female-founded product in the slice; likely documentation bias, flagged not fixed.
- **Geography:** strong EU/India representation (Belgium, Estonia, Germany, Netherlands, India, UK, Australia) but no Africa, LatAm, or SEA products; US covered only by Canny/Fillout.
- **Revenue opacity:** only Tally, Canny (to 2020), Featurebase, Upvoty, and Saber have founder-disclosed figures; Frill, Sleekplan, FeatureOS-current, and Nolt rely on secondary estimates or are UNKNOWN.
- **Failure cases:** Saber is the sole full-lifecycle/exit record; no dead-product post-mortems in the niche were included.
- **Aggregator pollution:** GetLatka/Tracxn founder and funding fields conflicted with primary sources twice (Frill CEO, FeatureOS founder, Nolt funding) — primaries prevailed throughout.

### Future Suggestions

1. **UserJot deep-dive:** revisit once founder identity and revenue are disclosable — unlimited-users-free-forever is the most aggressive pricing in the niche.
2. **Dead-pool slice:** post-mortems of shuttered feedback/form tools (e.g., competitors killed by Canny's bundling or Typeform's pricing) to complement this success-skewed slice.
3. **Metering-pricing experiment matrix:** compare conversion/support-load outcomes across tracked-user vs seat vs board vs response metering for a lab-built feedback widget.
4. **Micro-exit mechanics:** interview Saber-style buyers/sellers on deal structure (asset transfer, transition support, multiples) as a lab exit playbook.
5. **AI-triage build test:** replicate Featurebase's $0.49/resolution AI agent and Nolt's deliberate non-AI stance as an A/B product decision for lab tools.
