# Lessons for the Lab

TOP lessons from 100 indie products, each traced to records. These feed SYSTEM.md gates and future product decisions — they do not override them.

1. **Compound part-time before quitting** (Buttondown, Tally, SavvyCal): the lab's portfolio model matches how winners actually started — no quit-your-job mythology needed.
2. **Niche-down wedge beats feature parity** (SavvyCal vs Calendly, Buttondown vs Mailchimp): score "why would anyone switch" brutally at Gate 05.
3. **SEO owns the durable channel** (63/100): every product needs 1–3 ownable queries from day one — Phase 15 is not optional.
4. **Embed virality in the artifact** (SavvyCal credits, Buttondown footers): distribution designed into the product, not bolted on.
5. **Boring stack, solo-maintainable** (Photopea vanilla, Rails/Django/Next monocultures): Gate 11 should reject anything a solo dev can't run.
6. **Meter the expensive dimension** (Tally responses, analytics events): free-tier design is a survival skill — specify it in scope lock (Gate 07).
7. **Recurring or decay** (ShipFast −98% vs Gravity +price-ladder): one-time pricing needs an explicit renewal story before launch (Gate 24).
8. **Aggregators lie; primaries decide** (≥8 caught misattributions): Phase 02 triangulation rule is load-bearing, not decorative.
9. **Kill criteria need numbers** (ScreenshotOne's Max-MRR ceiling: new MRR ÷ churn): Gate 07 kill thresholds should be arithmetic, not vibes.
10. **Patience is a strategy** (Photopea 5 yrs unmonetized; Buttondown 4 yrs side): the portfolio can carry slow compounds — but only with dated review points (Phase 28), or patience becomes zombie-keeping.
